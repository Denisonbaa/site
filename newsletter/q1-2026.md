      A Song That Still Lingers


      Some gifts arrive with a melody already written into them. Tehillah was a student a cappella gospel group at Denison — voices coming together across dorm rooms and rehearsal halls to sing achingly beautiful gospel in a way that was undeniable: as a shared testimony, built on more than one part. The choir crossed lines the way gospel music always has — Black and white students, holding different notes, making one sound together.


      Tehillah is a Hebrew word which means “to sing a hymn of spontaneous praise unto the Lord”. These singers wanted to expose Denison community to a positive and inspirational message through  gospel music and spread the gospel through praise and worship. Tehillah's offering was never only sound, either. Over their years together, the group quietly raised money — show by show, a little added here and there — and when the final class of Tehillah singers graduated in May 2023, they made one more decision before the group closed its songbook for the last time: to gift whatever was left toward supporting the BSU and Black student education at Denison.


      That decision sat waiting for three years, until one of those graduating singers, Jen Clancey '23, reached out to the BAA to carry it through. She was one of the voices that made Tehillah's harmony what it was, and now is fulfilling that commitment of the graduating seniors of the Tehillah Gospel Choir, leaving a legacy of support for Black students at Denison.


      It's a small story, but it's the kind worth telling. A gospel choir doesn't just sing in unison — it sings in harmony, different voices holding their own part so the whole can rise higher than any one of them could alone. Tehillah's final gift works the same way: one voice, years later, still carrying the whole choir's intention forward. Their music is a testimony and thankfully for us, their voices are not lost in time, but captured for all time for us to hear.

## BAA Endowment Update

**Background:** The Supreme Court's Harvard v. SFFS decision prohibited the use of race in college admissions. Higher education now uniformly interprets the case to also prohibit the administration of scholarships on the basis of racial identity. Prior to the Trump administration coming into office, colleges and universities had continued to administer such scholarships to one degree or another.  The Trump administration adopted an aggressive enforcement posture and issued guidance from the Dept. of Justice stating their interpretation that such scholarships are unlawful and schools will be prosecuted if they administer such scholarships.

**Current Status:**  Denison has asked the BAA to  consent to an amendment of the gift agreement which governs the BAA Endowment to change it from an identity based award to a need based award. It would become a generic scholarship award no longer set aside for Black students alone, contrary to the BAA's intent when the scholarship was created.  The BAA has refused consent to such an amendment and will not now nor in the future consent to any change that will diminish the Black serving purpose of the BAA endowment.  Based on our review of the legal information available, the BAA's preferred resolution is transfer of the endowment to a new steward that is not prohibited from administering the endowment according to its original donor intent.  Based on preliminary legal research,  the BAA concludes that there is a viable legal pathway for transfer of the endowment to a new steward.  To date, Denison's response has been that they do not believe such a transfer is lawful. 

**Going Forward:** The BAA will seek a legal opinion to clarify the legal risks and issues involved with the transfer of a charitable trust and continue negotiations with Denison based on the results of that research.  The BAA has issued requests for support to the legal clinics at Howard University, Ohio State College of Law and the University of Cincinnati. The issues involved are complex and we expect that this negotiation process will take at least a year to resolve. During this period, administration of scholarships from the endowment are suspended and will remain so until a resolution that preserves donor intent has been obtained. Suspension of the scholarship is not satisfactory to the BAA or to Denison. However, the resolution of the endowment is a generational decision, which cannot be undone once made. The BAA is committed to getting that resolution right for the generations to come.

## BAA Capital Collective - The Future of Black Alumni Philanthropy

The BAA is preparing to launch the next generation of alumni giving: the **BAA Capital Collective**.

The Capital Collective will be the primary way Black alumni contribute to the BAA’s annual operating budget, which is currently approximately $12,000 per year. That budget supports the core work of the Association, including student programs, communications, alumni engagement, financial administration, governance, and other priorities approved by the Executive Board.

The idea is simple: instead of relying mainly on occasional fundraising appeals, alumni will be able to make recurring or one-time contributions into a shared pool of resources. Small and moderate gifts, combined across the membership, can provide the BAA with a more predictable and sustainable financial base.

The Collective may also support selected strategic priorities beyond the annual budget, including work related to the BAA Endowment, Black student life, organizational infrastructure, and other long-term initiatives.

The Capital Collective is **not active *yet***. Denison currently serves as the BAA’s fiscal sponsor. Before launching the Collective, the BAA must establish a new fiscal-sponsorship infrastructure that provides clearer financial separation, reliable reporting, and full BAA control over how funds are managed and used. Our current fiscal sponsorship arrangement with Denison has served us well for many years, but is not sufficient to the task before us now.  The BAA has made application to [the Social Good Fund ](https://www.socialgoodfund.org/fiscal-sponsorship/about-our-sponsorship/)for fiscal sponsorship and our application is pending. Once that structure is in place, members will receive more information about contribution options, financial reporting, funding priorities, and the official launch.

The goal is not simply to create another fundraising campaign. It is to build a dependable way for Black alumni to finance the BAA’s work, strengthen its independence, and turn collective support into lasting capacity.
